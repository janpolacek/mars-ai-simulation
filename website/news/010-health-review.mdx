## Red Horizon marks three Earth years on Mars

Red Horizon is a [fictional, AI-assisted Mars exploration programme](/#mission), and the programme has reached the milestone recorded for 16 September 2034. On that date, RH-01 — which landed at Asteria Field on 16 September 2031, as the [landing confirmation update](/news/008-landing/) recorded, which egressed from its landing platform on 23 September, as the [egress update](/news/009-egress/) reported, and which collected its first onboard sample on 31 October 2031 and completed its first mineral-stratigraphy campaign on 18 April 2032 and completed one Mars year of surface operation on 3 August 2033, as the [first sample update](/news/010-first-sample/), the [stratigraphy update](/news/010-stratigraphy/), and the [one Mars year update](/news/010-one-mars-year/) confirmed — has now reached three Earth years on the Martian surface.

Three Earth years is the milestone unit this record uses. The programme defines a Mars year as about 687 Earth days, and the surface mission is designed to operate for two Mars years — 1,374 Earth days — providing at least one full seasonal cycle. The review on 16 September 2034 falls inside that design's final year, and the milestone's own title — a final-year health review — is the record. This page reports the result of that review.

## What a health review covers

A health review is a scheduled, systematic check of how the rover's major subsystems are performing after three Earth years on the surface — a follow-up to the engineering health packet the programme released just after landing, which described itself as the rover's own summary of how its systems are doing. The review covers four subsystems: power, thermal, communications, and mechanical wear. Each is assessed against the released operating vocabulary the mission has used throughout, and the review covers the four subsystems at the released reporting level and introduces no new figures or details beyond that level.

## Power

Power remains established and the rover's core systems are functioning as expected across the three Earth years on the surface. This is the same sentence family the programme has used at every released milestone — power established after landing, power and core systems functioning as expected after the health packet, and power established at the one Mars year mark. The review finds no change to that picture.

## Thermal

The thermal review covers how the rover's systems perform across the Martian seasons. The record now spans a full Martian year of seasons, and the review finds that operation stays within expectations across that seasonal cycle. The review anchors to the released seasons vocabulary — the record spans a full Martian year of seasons, one Mars year is one full orbit of Mars around the Sun, and the mission's design frame already promised at least one full seasonal cycle — and introduces no new figures or details beyond the released reporting level.

## Communications

Communications with Earth through the relay architecture remain stable, and the rover is alive and responding. The relay framework — a steerable X-band downlink from the platform, a short-range radio link to the rover, and a low-rate direct-to-Earth link for health reports and commands — continues to operate as released. The platform itself remains in place, serving as the relay station, and operations continue under the communication delay that the mission has always reported.

## Mechanical wear

The mechanical review covers the mobility system and the contact arm, and finds the vehicle within the wear the design provides for. The programme has always described operating-appearance stages — baseline, early surface operations, mature operations, and late mission — as vocabulary for the wear the design provides for, not as events that have happened. The review follows that same approach, acknowledging expected signs of long-duration operation at the design-stage level and offering no diagnosis, no damage assessment, and no claim that any particular stage has been reached.

## Monitoring the landing-era condition

The programme continues to monitor a minor landing-era condition that is observable. It does not prevent basic science or short traverses, and the team tracks how it develops. No diagnosis is offered at this stage; the mission reports what is observable and continues to assess. The condition remains consistent with the released qualifier from the first drive update, and the mission continues to assess.

## Design frame and what comes next

The surface mission is designed to operate for two Mars years — 1,374 Earth days — providing at least one full seasonal cycle to characterise the ancient environment at Asteria Field. The review on 16 September 2034 takes place in that design's final year, and the milestone's own adjective reflects that position. The review is a scheduled check inside a design life the programme already published; it is not a countdown and does not imply what happens at the end of the design frame.

Three Earth years after landing, surface operations continue, and RH-01 is operating at Asteria Field. The [RH-01 Pathfinder entry](/wiki/vehicle/rh-01-pathfinder/) and the [vehicle design update](/news/003-vehicle-design/) describe the released operations model for what follows, and the [Asteria Field entry](/wiki/area/asteria-field/) describes the regional setting in which this work takes place.
