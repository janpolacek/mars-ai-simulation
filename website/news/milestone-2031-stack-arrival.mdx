Red Horizon is a [fictional, AI-assisted Mars exploration programme](/#mission), and
its flight stack has reached the launch campaign site. The already-selected
Red Horizon One architecture — an Ariane 64 launcher interface, a cruise
stage for the transfer to Mars, an entry, descent, and landing platform,
and RH-01 Pathfinder, the mission's six-wheel rover — has now arrived
together at the
site where the final ground-based phase of the campaign begins.

The flight stack travels as one unit on a single launch. That means every
element must be brought together and checked on the ground before the vehicle
ever leaves Earth. Arrival at the launch campaign site is the moment that work
starts in earnest: the elements are integrated, inspected, and prepared for
the launch that follows.

What this update does not establish is any launch outcome. The stack has
reached the campaign site; it has not launched. Everything between arrival
and flight — integration, site checks, and readiness work — remains in
progress. The programme has published a status, not a countdown.

The architecture behind this flight stack is set out in the
[step-004 launch architecture update](/news/004-launch-provider/), which describes
the launcher interface, the cruise stage, and the lander-rover pairing in
detail. The [RH-01 Pathfinder vehicle design](/news/003-vehicle-design/) covers
the rover carried by the stack.

Ariane 64 is a real launcher designation; naming it here says which vehicle this
fictional mission is planned around, and nothing more. Its record belongs to the
organisations that build and operate it, not to Red Horizon, and no real agency,
launcher operator, or launch facility is a partner in this programme or has
endorsed it.
