Red Horizon is a [fictional, AI-assisted Mars exploration programme](/#mission),
and the programme has reached the approach and arrival-preparation phase. On
21 August 2031, the [final approach correction update](/news/006-cruise-final-approach/)
closed the cruise record and promised the next status: the preparations for
reaching the destination. This update is that record — the first look at what
the programme is doing before entry, and what the team expects to learn next.

The destination is [Asteria Field](/wiki/area/asteria-field/),
named in the [project announcement](/news/001-project-announcement/),
already released as the mission's planned landing site. The spacecraft is
approaching, and the programme is preparing for the arrival phase that
follows the cruise.

## What will happen

The approach and arrival-preparation phase covers the work the mission does
before entry. The team completes entry, descent, and landing readiness checks;
the landing terrain and surface-science rationale are selected; and a landing
ellipse is named by the team. Throughout, the outcome stays uncertain — the
landing is confirmed only later, by communications.

The programme is not running a live countdown. These checks unfold on the
mission's own timeline, and the public record will report them as they develop.

## Which systems matter

The released mission architecture defines what is at stake. The landing platform
handles entry, descent, landing, rover deployment, and surface relay; the rover
is carried to the surface and deploys when checks are complete. The rover talks
to the platform over a short-range radio link, while the platform returns data to
Earth over a steerable X-band downlink, and the rover keeps a low-rate
direct-to-Earth health link. [The launch architecture update](/news/004-launch-provider/)
describes this framework in full.

Each of these systems must be verified as the mission transitions from cruise to
approach: navigation must hold the track, communications must remain stable
across the deep-space link, and the platform and the [rover](/wiki/vehicle/rh-01-pathfinder/)
must be ready for the sequence ahead.

## What the team will know first

The first signals the team looks for are the ones that confirm the spacecraft
and its systems have made it through the approach phase unchanged: whether
navigation held the planned track, whether communications remained healthy
throughout, and whether the platform and rover responded as designed when
exercised. The programme reports these readiness-check results before anything
else — the landing itself is confirmed only later, by communications, and that
confirmation belongs to the next record.

## When another update will follow

The programme will report the outcome of entry and landing when communications
establish it, in a later record. No date and no success claim are part of this
update; the landing remains unconfirmed until the next status reports it.
