## Landing confirmed at Asteria Field

Red Horizon is a [fictional, AI-assisted Mars exploration programme](/#mission),
and the programme reached the milestone recorded for 16 September 2031. On that
date, Red Horizon One — launched 17 February 2031 aboard Ariane 64 and now in cruise
since the mission's mid-course corrections — entered the Martian atmosphere and
confirmed landing at Asteria Field, the mission's destination. The [final approach
correction update](/news/006-cruise-final-approach/) closed the cruise record with the
acknowledgement that details of entry, landing, or surface operations were not part of
that record; this update marks where the landing record begins.

## What is confirmed

The first confirmations from the surface are the ones the programme considers the
foundation of a successful landing: power is established, communications with Earth
through the relay architecture are stable, and the rover is alive and responding.
These three factors — power, communications, and the rover responding — are what the
programme reports before describing the landing as successful. The [launch
architecture update](/news/004-launch-provider/) describes the relay framework the
mission relies on: the platform stays where it lands and returns data to Earth over a
steerable X-band downlink, while the rover communicates over a short-range link and
maintains a low-rate direct-to-Earth health connection.

## What remains to be checked

Before the mission can describe the surface phase as fully underway, several items
remain to be checked. Commissioning of the rover's instruments and systems is the
first step; sensors will be assessed next; and the ramps will be checked before the
rover leaves the platform. The local environment must also be assessed before any
surface activity proceeds. These items are reported as a checklist of what comes next, not as
results — the programme is still in the earliest hours of its surface life.

## First expected surface operations

The first expected surface operations follow the released mission architecture.
Before the rover leaves the platform, platform and system checks are carried out.
The [Asteria Field wiki](/wiki/area/asteria-field/) and the [RH-01
Pathfinder](/wiki/vehicle/rh-01-pathfinder/) entries describe the released vocabulary
for this phase: the rover is carried to the surface and the plan is for it to deploy
from the platform when the checks are complete. No surface activity beyond those
checks is reported here.

## An open question

Whether the commissioning sequence will complete entirely on the nominal plan
remains an open question, and the programme will report on how it develops in its
next record. The [final approach update](/news/006-cruise-final-approach/) closed the
cruise phase by noting that entry, landing, and surface operations would be reported
in due course; that reporting now begins with this update.

## What the record covers

This record covers the confirmed landing at Asteria Field on 16 September 2031 and
the immediately following commissioning period. Later surface milestones belong to
subsequent updates that have not yet been released.
