## Red Horizon completes two Mars years at Asteria Field

Red Horizon is a [fictional, AI-assisted Mars exploration programme](/#mission), and the programme has reached the milestone recorded for 21 June 2035. On that date, RH-01 — which landed at Asteria Field on 16 September 2031, as the [landing confirmation update](/news/008-landing/) recorded, which egressed from its landing platform on 23 September, as the [egress update](/news/009-egress/) reported, which collected its first onboard sample on 31 October 2031 and completed its first mineral-stratigraphy campaign on 18 April 2032, as the [first sample update](/news/010-first-sample/) and the [stratigraphy update](/news/010-stratigraphy/) confirmed, and which completed one Mars year of surface operation on 3 August 2033, as the [one Mars year update](/news/010-one-mars-year/) recorded — has now completed two Mars years of surface operation.

Two Mars years is the design life the programme published at its outset: a Mars year is about 687 Earth days, so the surface mission is designed to operate for two Mars years — roughly 1,374 Earth days — providing at least one full seasonal cycle to characterise the ancient environment at Asteria Field. The milestone of 21 June 2035 is that published design life now completed. With supported surface operations ended, a 30-day listening campaign begins as closeout — the team continues to listen for any further signal from the rover rather than commanding surface work. This campaign is closeout, not an extension of surface science.

## The design frame completed

The programme told the public, from the outset, that the surface mission is designed to operate for two Mars years — 1,374 Earth days — providing at least one full seasonal cycle to characterise the ancient environment at Asteria Field. That figure appeared in the [project announcement update](/news/001-project-announcement/), in the [Red Horizon programme entry](/wiki/project/red-horizon/), and was reaffirmed at each milestone along the way: the one Mars year update recorded the first year completed, and the final-year health review placed itself inside the design's final year without implying what happens at its end.

The milestone of 21 June 2035 completes that frame. Two Mars years of surface operation are now complete — a full run of the surface mission the programme already told the public it would attempt. The record marks the design life as achieved, measured against what was published, and does not extend into what follows it.

## The record so far

The two Mars years recorded here span the full surface mission as it has been publicly documented. RH-01 landed at Asteria Field on 16 September 2031 with power established and communications through the relay architecture stable, as the [landing confirmation update](/news/008-landing/) reported. The rover egressed to the surface on 23 September, as the [egress update](/news/009-egress/) confirmed, and took its first controlled drive on 30 September, navigating limited power, uneven terrain, and the communication delay.

The mission's scientific work proceeded across two further milestones. The [first sample update](/news/010-first-sample/) recorded the first onboard sample collection and mineral analysis on 31 October 2031, and the [stratigraphy update](/news/010-stratigraphy/) reported the first mineral-stratigraphy campaign completed on 18 April 2032 — layered outcrop observations across Asteria Field, read together with mineral and composition data into a grounded sedimentary interpretation. One Mars year of surface operation was completed on 3 August 2033, and a final-year health review of power, thermal, communications, and mechanical wear was reported on 16 September 2034, as the [health review update](/news/010-health-review/) recorded.

## Science gathered so far

The science recorded across the two Mars years is drawn from the released milestones above. The [first sample update](/news/010-first-sample/) reported the first onboard sample collection and mineral analysis: the contact tool abraded a weathered surface, captured no more than one cubic centimetre of loose material, analysed it inside the tool's reusable chamber, and returned the material to the ground near its source. The mineral analysis was presented at the cautious level the mission uses throughout — the spectrum is consistent with the mineral composition expected from the weathered terrain, and the team is checking the result against further observations.

The [stratigraphy update](/news/010-stratigraphy/) reported the first mineral-stratigraphy campaign: layered outcrop observations across Asteria Field, read together with mineral and composition data into a grounded sedimentary interpretation. That interpretation carries the mission's standing stated limitation — no target at Asteria Field is described as confirmed clay, confirmed sulfate, habitable, or fossil-bearing, and no confirmed mineral detection or confirmed ancient-lake claim is made. The result is one completed campaign of observations checked against further data, leading to a cautious interpretation.

The two Mars years do not add new science results beyond these released records. The mission's approach has been modest and cautious from the outset: observe, analyse, check, and report what the evidence supports — and name what it does not.

## Listening campaign begins as closeout

With supported surface operations ended, the mission enters a listening campaign as closeout. The team continues to listen for any further signal from the rover rather than commanding surface work. The campaign is closeout — not an extension of surface science, and no further surface operations are commanded — and marks the transition from commanding the rover to monitoring it.

The listening continues through the relay architecture the programme has used throughout: a steerable downlink from the platform, a short-range link to the rover, and a low-rate direct-to-Earth connection for health reports and commands. The platform remains in place, serving as the relay station. The rover's systems are monitored at the same level the programme has reported at every milestone since landing.

## What comes next

The listening campaign is the mission's next phase on this record. The programme will continue to monitor the rover and listen for any further signal, operating at the same released reporting level that has characterised every milestone so far.

The [RH-01 Pathfinder entry](/wiki/vehicle/rh-01-pathfinder/) and the [vehicle design update](/news/003-vehicle-design/) describe the released operating-appearance stages — baseline, early surface operations, mature operations, and late mission — as vocabulary for the wear the design provides for, not as events that have happened.
