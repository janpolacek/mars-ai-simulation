Red Horizon is a [fictional, AI-assisted Mars exploration programme](/#mission), and
the programme has reached its first planned trajectory-correction opportunity. On
3 March 2031, Red Horizon One — already in interplanetary cruise since its 17 February
2031 launch aboard Ariane 64 — carried out the first scheduled course-refinement
manoeuvre of its journey toward Mars. The [launch update](/news/005-launch/)
records the mission's departure and the naming of this opportunity.

A trajectory-correction opportunity is a planned manoeuvre to refine the spacecraft's
course toward its destination. During the long cruise phase, the mission's trajectory
is not perfectly fixed at launch; small uncertainties accumulate over weeks of travel.
At designated intervals, the spacecraft fires its engines in a carefully planned burst
to adjust its path, fine-tuning the route so it arrives at Mars on the intended
trajectory. The same navigation systems that guided the spacecraft out of Earth's
gravity well now take on the harder job of steering across millions of kilometres of
interplanetary space.

The 3 March opportunity tested whether the cruise stage has placed the stack on the
correct course for Mars, and whether the navigation and communications systems are
performing as designed during the long cruise phase. The programme reports that the
opportunity was carried out and assessed within the planned frame: routine operations
have held, and the spacecraft is tracking as expected. No course-state claim beyond
that released framing is part of this record, and the opportunity
discloses no figures or measurements of any kind.

Behind the scenes, cruise operations continue in the routine way the programme has
described: the flight stage handles navigation, thermal control, and communications
with Earth; instruments are exercised and checked out; and the mission's teams monitor
the spacecraft's health from across the deep-space link. That transition from ground
control to autonomous deep-space operations — already underway since launch — means
the mission's own team carries full responsibility for the vehicle's journey, with
limited ability to intervene physically.

The next status the programme expects to report is a cruise checkout around 20 May
2031, covering the health of all flight elements. The mission remains in cruise, and
arrival at Asteria Field lies ahead.
