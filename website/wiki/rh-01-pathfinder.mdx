## Identity

RH-01 Pathfinder is Red Horizon One's surface rover, on the first mission of the Red Horizon
programme: a compact robotic Mars surveyor that maps local terrain, investigates rocks in
context, and establishes safe routes for later science targets. Its designation is RH-01. It is a
surface survey rover — an instrument that has been made mobile, not a car, a miniature
tank, a spaceplane, or a crew vehicle.

The design is frozen, and it reflects the constraints of the surface rather than styling
preferences: dust, uneven terrain, limited power, the communication delay between the rover and the
people operating it, and the fact that every decision on the surface is made remotely. The frozen
design is the one set out in the programme's
[vehicle design briefing](/news/003-vehicle-design/); the design dossier behind it is
the source for this page.

## Visual identity and silhouette

### Proportions and silhouette

The rover's proportions are roughly two metres in class. The body is low and broad rather than tall
and narrow, with its equipment mass visually concentrated close to the chassis. Its corners are
protected and modestly rounded, but the overall form stays rectilinear enough to show where the
sealed body ends and the mobility hardware begins. The front is the science end and carries the
stowed contact arm; the rear is a quiet service end, and front-to-rear orientation is consistent,
with the arm fixed to the front-centre of the body.

### Body and colour blocking

The primary equipment housing is warm white with a matte, ceramic-like finish: fine surface
variation, softly visible edges, and enough tonal separation from a neutral background to keep the
body readable. A small number of muted red identification panels provide the Red Horizon
connection without turning the rover into a painted mascot — the red is restrained, opaque, and
integrated into the body panels rather than applied as racing stripes, warning graphics, or a large
logo.

The upper equipment deck is dark and flat. It gives the pale body a visual counterweight and makes
the mast mount easy to locate, and it is not a solar panel field, a cockpit roof, or an open
platform. It carries no text, national flag, official agency insignia, or invented technical label.
Body panels meet in narrow, controlled seams, and recessed fasteners and small service boundaries
are visible where they explain how the machine is assembled and maintained. The body has no
windows, transparent covers, passenger hatch, cockpit, air intake, decorative fins, exposed fuel
tanks, or loose external wiring; any dark region has a mechanical explanation such as a deck, pivot
cover, hub, lens, or protected recess.

### Six-wheel mobility system

The lower structure is the rover's defining engineering feature. RH-01 has exactly six broad
open-mesh wheels: a front, middle, and rear wheel on the left side and a corresponding front,
middle, and rear wheel on the right. The six-wheel arrangement is fixed — there is no seventh
support wheel, no hidden auxiliary roller, no duplicate wheel, no track assembly, and no leg
substituted for a wheel.

Each wheel is a real load-bearing element with a clear circular hub, a broad mesh or perforated
metal face, and a dark inner structure; the mesh reads as engineered wheel material rather than a
bicycle spoke pattern, solid tyre tread, or a toy-like plastic lattice. The wheel diameter suits
the compact body and the irregular gravel of the Martian surface. The wheels connect to the body
through visible articulated suspension, whose links, pivots, and hubs form continuous mechanical
chains rather than isolated bars that disappear into the chassis, and whose travel provides enough
clearance for the wheels to pass over small stones without the body resting on the ground. All six
positions stay readable from multiple angles, each wheel sitting at a plausible working height with
grounded contact.

### Mast and vision system

The mast is short, central, and restrained. It rises from a compact mount on the dark instrument
deck and carries one horizontal stereo-camera housing, which holds a closely spaced pair of dark
lenses — a clear forward-looking vision system that does not make the mast resemble a head. It is
not a tower: there is no second camera tower, no arbitrary antenna cluster, no dish, no radar boom,
and no floating sensor, and the mast's support, camera housing, and cable routing are integrated
into the body so the wiring stays protected from the environment.

### Front contact arm and travel cradle

RH-01 carries one front-centre contact arm for close inspection of rocks and soil. It has exactly
two connected joints and ends in one small contact tool, and its scale matches the compact rover —
it is not an oversized excavator, crane, drill mast, weapon, or articulated decoration.

In the baseline travel configuration the arm's two links nest inside a shallow travel cradle built
into the front body. The cradle supports the links along their length, the small tool is secured
within the protected outline, and the whole assembly stays below the visual height of the mast
base; no link is raised vertically, stretched away from the rover, or left dangling toward the
floor. The arm is one continuous two-joint mechanism from its front mount to its retained tool. The
cradle is a practical restraint that protects the arm while the rover drives, it is distinct from
the science arm, and it contains no second instrument.

### Rear, materials, and surface character

The rear of RH-01 is intentionally uneventful: a plain sealed service panel with no second science
arm, exhaust assembly, cargo rack, ladder, or ornamental engine detail. Its quiet geometry is a
stable orientation landmark when the rover is viewed from behind.

Materials suggest a real field machine rather than a showpiece: matte ceramic-coated body panels,
subdued brushed metal on the arm, dark protected pivots, durable wheel mesh, and small recessed
fasteners, with soft and physically restrained reflections.

### Baseline and operating appearance

In its baseline configuration the vehicle is clean and new, with machining marks and slight
material variation but no accumulated Mars dust, impact damage, missing panels, burn marks, or
dramatic wear. The baseline studio appearance places the full-size vehicle on a seamless neutral
white floor with a white background, under broad soft light, with a faint grounded shadow: an
engineering reference rather than a scene, with no rocks, red soil, landing platform, astronaut,
smoke, dust plume, captions, or graphic overlay.

The dossier defines four operating-appearance stages — baseline; early surface operations, with
restrained dust and short wheel tracks; mature operations, with naturally accumulated dust, faded
paint, rubbed wheel surfaces, and small abrasion; and late mission, where wear reflects the rover's
operational history while the maintained scientific machine remains recognisable and functional.
They describe the wear the design provides for, not events that have happened.

## Science and operations

The rover's payload covers the scope the programme set out in its
[second public step](/news/002-payload-selection/): panorama and navigation imaging, local weather
observations, mineral and rock-context sensing, and a close-contact tool. Its science role is
observational and methodical — it gathers evidence before an interpretation is made.

Operations follow short, deliberate traverses: survey the terrain, select a target, approach it,
perform contact and context observations, record the results, and choose the next safe route.
Mobility exists to put instruments in front of the right ground.

## Relationship to the mission

RH-01 Pathfinder is Red Horizon One's surface rover, and it works at the scale of individual rocks
and patches of soil. Its mobility and its instrument set serve one purpose: to
place the mission's measurements in front of the right ground and to record enough context for
those measurements to mean something. What the rover *is* — its designation, its silhouette, its
six-wheel mobility, and the payload it carries — is the subject of this page.

Red Horizon is a fictional, AI-assisted Mars exploration programme, and RH-01 Pathfinder is a
fictional vehicle described here from the programme's own design dossier. The studio reference on
this page is illustrative artwork, not mission photography, and no real space agency is named or
implied.
