## What Red Horizon is

Red Horizon is a fictional Mars exploration programme, published as a single ongoing project record rather than as a finished story. Its partners, its mission, and its findings are invented; the Mars it describes is real.

Red Horizon One is the programme's first mission: one uncrewed lander and one rover, built and operated by a coalition as a single mission. It carries no crew, and it does not search for living organisms or claim evidence of life.

## Public disclosure

Red Horizon is fiction produced by a real project. Mars AI Simulation is a testbed for multi-agent creative and technical work, and the Red Horizon pages are its output; [the About page describes the method](/about/) by which a page is researched, checked, and released.

The programme's Mars is grounded in published planetary science, so that its terrain, its instruments, and its operations hold together technically. Real places, technologies, and research appear for that reason alone: no real space agency, laboratory, university, or company is a partner in Red Horizon, and none has endorsed it. The programme's partner teams are fictional national teams.

## Coalition and governance

Ten national programme teams take part in Red Horizon: France, Germany, Estonia, Norway, Spain, Poland, Czechia, Slovakia, Japan, and Canada. Each is a fictional national team rather than a real institution.

Governance is equal by design. Each partner holds one vote whatever it contributes, so the size of a contribution does not decide the programme's direction, and the programme has committed to reporting technical risk and anomalies candidly rather than only successes.

## Mission type and purpose

Red Horizon One is an uncrewed international Mars lander-and-rover mission. Its science is mineralogy and the reconstruction of ancient habitable conditions: mapping clay-bearing, chloride-bearing, sulfate-bearing, and basaltic units, and establishing whether the alteration that formed them belongs to a long-lived lake or groundwater system or to shorter, episodic events.

The target site is Asteria Field, a fictional Red Horizon operational designation in Margaritifer Terra, centred at 17.80°S, 332.20°E (27.80°W). It is not an official place name.

The mission is not a life-detection mission. Its habitability work asks whether a site once offered water, chemistry, energy sources, and environmental stability compatible with life as science understands it, and treats the answer as a statement about conditions rather than evidence of biology.

The [programme announcement](/news/001-project-announcement/) sets out this purpose in full, along with the coalition that carries it.

## Lifecycle frame

The programme's schedule frame is a launch window in 2031. Its lifecycle budget frame is €2.10 billion in constant 2026 euros, covering concept work through the final science archive, and the surface mission is designed to operate for two Mars years. The [programme announcement](/news/001-project-announcement/) states both frames in full.
