# 001 — Project announcement

## Private situation

Research leaders identify a gap between short Mars demonstrations and sustained,
openly documented surface science. They begin informal conversations about a joint
robotic programme that combines geology, local environmental sensing, and durable
rover operations. No mission is funded or technically frozen yet.

The proposal then becomes a named programme. The partner groups agree to share a
mission architecture, science access, and public communications. They divide broad
responsibilities among spacecraft systems, instruments, ground operations, and data
stewardship, and retain an independent technical review.

## Constraints and decisions

- The programme wants a visibly multinational identity without implying that real
  agencies have committed money or endorsed the fictional mission.
- The first mission must be narrow enough to be credible: one rover, one landing
  site, and a science package that earns later expansion.
- Create fictional coalition/member names before public publication if real agency
  roles would imply endorsement.
- Governance must allow candid reporting of risk and anomalies.
- Exact funding numbers and contractual details are unnecessary unless the human
  explicitly chooses to make them part of the fiction.

## Allowed public angle

Red Horizon may now be announced publicly, with its shared scientific purpose, its
partner groups, the target region, the science objective, and the launch window and
lifecycle budget frame as locked in `docs/scenario.md`. The announcement may state
who, where, why, and the price and schedule frame, and may establish recurring
organisational names. Use the window and year only; the exact launch and landing
dates wait for their authorised release steps.

## Spoilers and handoff

Do not name the final payload or instrument selection, which contributor owns which
hardware, or the launch provider; those decisions belong to step 002 and later.
Candidate media: programme mark, partner-map graphic, or a simple technical planning
visual. Dependency: none — this is the first public step.
