## Executive brief

Asteria Field is the proposed first Red Horizon operating region for RH-01
Pathfinder: a compact, unmanned survey rover intended to map local terrain,
inspect rocks in context, and establish safe routes. The region is designed as
an ancient highland-to-basin transition with three mutually useful evidence
classes: resistant cratered basement, fine-grained or layered basin-margin
material, and a shallow channel/bench system that can be tested for signs of
past water alteration.

The scenario deliberately asks the rover to build a chain of evidence rather
than announce a discovery. Orbital context selects the region; surface
observations test texture, layering, chemistry, and relationships between
units. No part of the dossier claims present water, life, or a confirmed
mineral detection at Asteria Field.

## Real Mars context and scenario boundary

### What is real

- **Margaritifer Terra is a real, officially catalogued Martian terra.** The
  USGS Gazetteer of Planetary Nomenclature lists it as a feature on Mars; the
  Gazetteer record is the authority for the parent name, not for Asteria Field.
  [USGS Gazetteer: Margaritifer Terra](https://planetarynames.wr.usgs.gov/Feature/3701)
- Regional geologic maps describe Margaritifer Terra as preserving ancient
  cratered highlands, impact-basin structures, valley networks, sedimentary and
  alluvial deposits, volcanic and tectonic units, and later eolian modification.
  [USGS SIM 3209](https://www.usgs.gov/maps/geologic-map-mtm-15027-20027-25027-and-25032-quadrangles-margaritifer-terra-region-mars)
- The Margaritifer basin / Morava–Ladon system records a long, complex history
  of fluvial incision, basin sedimentation, possible lacustrine plains,
  volcanic resurfacing, chaotic terrain, and later mass wasting and wind-borne
  redistribution. These are regional interpretations, not a description of
  the invented local units below.
  [USGS SIM 3489](https://www.usgs.gov/maps/geologic-map-mtm-10022-and-15022-quadrangles-morava-valles-and-margaritifer-basin-mars)
- Orbital spectroscopy has identified clay and other hydrated minerals in
  multiple Martian regions; clay and sulfate minerals are useful records of
  past environmental conditions. Asteria's mineral targets borrow this
  scientific rationale but do not inherit a confirmed detection.
  [NASA: Regions of Mars with clays and hydrated minerals](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/regions-of-mars-with-clays-and-hydrated-minerals-identified-from-orbit/)

## Geographic setting

The planning envelope is placed within the latitude/longitude span of
the southwestern Margaritifer Terra mapping context used by USGS SIM 3209. The
fictional field is intentionally not placed on a named crater, vallis, or
official landing-site feature. It is a transition zone in the scenario:

1. a gently elevated, heavily cratered highland surface to the west;
2. a shallow, arcuate basin-margin depression to the east; and
3. a narrow set of paleochannel benches trending from the northwest toward the
   southeast.

This arrangement gives RH-01 a safe reconnaissance surface and nearby science
targets without requiring an implausibly long first traverse. The regional
plate is a relationship diagram: use it to keep the fictional geography
consistent, not to infer real local geology.

## Working geological history

The labels below are a scenario stratigraphy for mission planning. “Unit” means
an operational mapping class, not a newly discovered or officially mapped
Martian formation.

| Sequence | Scenario unit                        | Working interpretation                                                                        | Why RH-01 cares                                                              |
| -------- | ------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1        | AF-H0 · cratered highland basement   | Resistant, dark-to-neutral fractured bedrock with scattered blocky ejecta                     | Baseline for texture, abrasion, and unaltered-versus-altered comparison      |
| 2        | AF-B1 · basin-margin bench           | Laterally persistent, finer-grained bench material accumulated against the shallow depression | Tests whether fine sediment records transport and aqueous alteration         |
| 3        | AF-C2 · paleochannel fill            | Discontinuous low-relief channel and bar-like surfaces crossing AF-H0/AF-B1                   | Tests flow direction, sorting, rounding, and contact relationships           |
| 4        | AF-L3 · light-toned layered exposure | Eroded, thin-bedded exposure on the northwestern bench                                        | Highest-priority context target for hydration and stratigraphic observations |
| 5        | AF-E4 · young eolian mantle          | Patchy wind-redistributed dust and sand draped over older units                               | Operational nuisance and a control on how much primary material is exposed   |

The scenario history is: ancient basement formation and impact modification;
later weathering and incision; deposition of basin-margin and channel material;
localized preservation of layered exposures; then long-term degradation,
impact gardening, and eolian mantling. The ordering is deliberately compatible
with the broad regional history in the USGS maps while remaining an invented
local hypothesis.

## Mineralogy and ancient-habitability rationale

### Testable targets

- **Phyllosilicate-bearing alteration:** test fine-grained AF-B1 and the lower
  beds of AF-L3 for clay-like alteration products and compare them with AF-H0.
- **Sulfate or other hydrated phases:** test paleochannel margins and evaporitic-
  looking pockets only as hypotheses; use the rover's local measurements to
  distinguish a target from a visual resemblance.
- **Iron-bearing phases:** use colour, texture, and instrument response to test
  oxidation gradients across fresh-looking blocks, weathered surfaces, and
  layered material.
- **Texture and provenance:** record grain size, sorting, clast shape, bedding,
  cross-cutting relations, and contact geometry before assigning an origin.

### Why this could preserve habitability evidence

The useful scenario combination is not “water was here, therefore life.” It is
the possible co-location of fine sediment, alteration minerals, and protected
layer contacts. Those materials could preserve environmental information from
an early Mars surface that was more geologically active. The operational goal is
to determine whether the materials are compatible with aqueous alteration and
deposition, and how confidently that interpretation can be made from context.

### Guardrails

No Asteria target is a biosignature claim. No target is “confirmed clay,”
“confirmed sulfate,” “habitable,” or “fossil-bearing” until a later approved
science interpretation says so. The first mission phase uses “candidate,”
“possible,” and “test” language.

## Terrain and landing design

### Landing ellipse

The primary scenario ellipse is **6.4 × 3.2 km**, long axis 078°/258°
(roughly east-northeast to west-southwest), centred on 17.80°S, 332.20°E. A
2.4 × 1.2 km inner touchdown zone is held near the western half of the ellipse
to preserve a low-slope exit onto AF-H0. The eastern half remains a contingency
area only because it approaches the bench break.

The ellipse is a planning geometry, not a flight-certified dispersion product.
It assumes a landing solution accurate enough to keep the first drive within the
mapped operations frame; the actual flight system, atmospheric state, and
landing dispersions are outside this dossier.

### Terrain classes

| Class            | Scenario character                                           | RH-01 operating rule                                            |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| H0 open highland | Firm-looking gravel and scattered blocks, 0–8° nominal slope | Preferred first-drive surface; inspect before committing        |
| B1 bench         | Fine mantling over weak-looking ledges, 2–10°                | Cross only on mapped bench lines; avoid unverified edges        |
| C2 channel floor | Low relief, loose sediment, local wheel-trap pockets         | Enter only after imaging; use short, reversible approach        |
| L3 layered scarp | Benches and short scarps, 8–16° local slopes                 | Science stop from the toe or shoulder; no direct scarp ascent   |
| E4 mantle        | Dust ripples and sand sheets, variable bearing strength      | Use as a hazard overlay; do not infer bedrock from colour alone |

Nominal rover driving stays at or below 12° sustained slope, with a 15°
scenario stop threshold. A wheel-slip or tilt trend, a continuous loose mantle,
or an unverified ledge overrides the nominal limit. RH-01 does not cross the
layered scarp face; observations are made from stable shoulders and toes.

## Engineering hazards

1. **Mantle over hardpan:** E4 can hide a firm-looking but low-bearing pocket;
   approach obliquely, use wheel-track observations, and stop if slip increases.
2. **Block fields:** AF-H0 ejecta blocks can narrow the wheel path and threaten
   the low chassis; route around clusters rather than climbing individual blocks.
3. **Bench breaks:** AF-B1/C2 contacts may create short ledges; keep a 75 m
   scenario stand-off from unverified breaks and image the route from above.
4. **Cross-slope exposure:** the northern bench can place one side of the rover
   near a drop; maintain the mapped contour route and reject sharp turns.
5. **Dust and visibility:** local dust movement can erase tracks and reduce
   image contrast; preserve a return route before entering a low-contrast patch.

The 75 m stand-off, 12° drive limit, and 15° stop threshold are scenario
controls. They are not RH-01 flight or mobility specifications.

## Science zones and traverse logic

| ID | Scenario zone        | Location / approach                          | Science objective                                                         | Priority  |
| -- | -------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------- |
| P0 | Inner touchdown zone | Western half of ellipse                      | Health check, horizon panorama, wheel-soil baseline                       | Mandatory |
| S1 | Paleochannel Bench   | 2.6 km ENE of P0; approach along H0 shoulder | Channel geometry, sorting, clast shape, alteration at contacts            | 1         |
| S2 | Layered Exposure     | 3.8 km NNW of P0; stop at southern shoulder  | Bedding, colour/texture changes, hydrated-mineral candidate sampling      | 2         |
| S3 | Crater-fill Contact  | 2.9 km SSE of P0; approach on western rim    | Compare fine fill with H0 basement and test oxidation/alteration gradient | 3         |
| S4 | Highlands Control    | 1.4 km W of P0                               | Fresh-looking control surface for regional comparison                     | 1A        |

Baseline sequence is **P0 → S4 → S1 → S2**, with **S3** added only if the
return route remains demonstrably safe. The first science loop is approximately
11.2 km of planned path in the scenario map, excluding pauses, reversals, and
contingency detours. A shorter safe loop, **P0 → S4 → S1 → P0**, is the default
fallback. All distances are map-design values, not a claimed vehicle range.

The science order protects interpretation: S4 provides a local control before
the rover enters the channel/bench system; S1 establishes process context; S2
tests whether layered material carries a distinct alteration history; S3 is a
comparison target rather than a required “discovery” stop.

## Communications and operations

The scenario assumes no permanent surface relay at Asteria Field. RH-01 stores
observations, receives a bounded command sequence, and uses scheduled relay or
direct-to-orbiter opportunities when available in the wider Red Horizon
architecture. A high western shoulder is reserved as a communications-safe
pause point; the low channel floor is not used for long command sequences until
the link geometry is checked.

Every traverse leg ends with a panorama, route-state record, and a return-path
decision. A communications outage does not trigger an improvised drive. The
rover pauses in place, preserves power and thermal margins, and resumes only
after an approved sequence is available. Exact orbit, antenna, latency, and
power budgets are separate vehicle/mission decisions and must not be invented
from this regional dossier.
